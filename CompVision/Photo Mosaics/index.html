Calculating a Homography Matrix

I worked on computing a homography matrix using at least four corresponding points between images.

I solved it using least squares.

I used these points: [[106,78],[45,217],[371,213],[315,51]] corresponding to [[0,0],[0,301],[402,301],[402,0]] (noted from my work in the next section).

This created the following system of linear equations: \[ \begin{bmatrix} 106 & 78 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 106 & 78 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 45 & 217 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 45 & 217 & 1 & -13545 & -65317 \\ 371 & 213 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & -149142 & -85626 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 371 & 213 & 1 & -111671 & -64113 \\ 315 & 51 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & -126630 & -20502 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 315 & 51 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} h1 \\ h2 \\ h3 \\ h4 \\ h5 \\ h6 \\ h7 \\ h8 \\ \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 301 \\ 402 \\ 301 \\ 402 \\ 0 \\ \end{bmatrix} \]

The resulting homography matrix is: \[ H = \begin{bmatrix} 4.57035247e+00 & 2.00569425e+00 & -6.40901513e+02 \\ 8.47015970e-01 & 6.55653103e+00 & -6.01193113e+02 \\ 2.64495777e-03 & 8.00501991e-03 & 1.00000000e+00 \\ \end{bmatrix} \]

Reading the entries in this matrix, it seems very likely that my program computed the homography correctly

Rectifying a Side-Angle Photo

I tried correcting a photo of a square taken from an angle to make it look like it was shot straight-on.

I tested two methods: Bilinear Interpolation (rectified_BI) and Nearest Neighbor (rectified_NN).

Here are the results (NOTE: CLEAN UP PRESENTATION):

Rectification 1
Rectification 2

The hardest part was setting up the canvas. At first, only the square was rectified, and the rest of the image was cropped out.

To address this, I mapped the square’s four corners to a new rectangle ([[0,0],[0,301],[402,301],[402,0]]), choosing these coordinates to match the image size (302x403) for simplicity, without preserving aspect ratio. Then, I calculated where the homography mapped the original image’s corners and created a canvas large enough to fit them. I also translated coordinates to handle negative values, ensuring the entire image fit within the canvas.

Building Panoramas

I worked on combining multiple images taken from the same position into seamless panoramas.

I created three panoramas, each from two images, using both Bilinear Interpolation (pano_BI) and Nearest Neighbor (pano_NN) methods.

Here are the results (NOTE: CLEAN UP PRESENTATION):

Panorama 1
Panorama 2
Panorama 3

The biggest challenge was the canvas size. I started with a large canvas, padding the base image by 500 pixels on all sides. I picked corresponding points between images, computed the homography, and mapped the second image onto a separate canvas of the same size. Then, I combined the canvases.

Managing alpha values was complex. I created an alpha_values matrix to track pixel placement, setting values to 1 for the base image and adding 1 for each warped image pixel. I combined the canvases with an elementwise sum, divided by the alpha matrix to blend overlaps (NumPy handled zero-division errors automatically).

Matching Features Between Images

I matched feature descriptors between image pairs using Lowe’s ratio test, computing nearest neighbors and thresholding based on the ratio of first to second nearest neighbor distances.

Robust Homography with RANSAC

I implemented 4-point RANSAC from scratch to estimate homographies from matched features, then used them to stitch images automatically, building on my earlier mosaicing work.
